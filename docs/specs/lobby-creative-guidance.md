# Lobby chat as creative guidance

Implemented locally October 21, 2026. Pipeline 4, prompt 12, schema 2. Production deployment and human playtesting remain separate.

## Player experience

Players can write suggestions and optionally select **Use this idea** on any message. Support is per member and idempotent; removing your reaction leaves everyone else’s intact. The inline chat shows marked suggestions before start. This preview contains the actual messages, not an AI summary. Unmarked suggestions are still considered.

Starting freezes the conversation and reactions. **Gathering your ideas** precedes world creation when there are messages. **Your ideas in this set** shows the extracted preferences, source quotes, distinct supporter counts, unresolved disagreements, and explanations for adaptations and omissions. It appears during generation and in the setting information after packs arrive. It exposes no future cards. Only lobby members can read it.

## Pipeline contract

1. `lobby/worldbuilding.ts` snapshots the entire bounded conversation: at most 200 messages of 500 characters. Chronological `m1` source IDs and anonymous `p1` participant IDs preserve attribution without sending account IDs. Supporters remain distinct. The previous last-40/6,000-character selection applies only to the legacy pack path.
2. A Luna **interpret** job receives that snapshot as untrusted data, plus the fixed power and format constraints. It extracts at most forty preferences (premise, subject, tone, avoidance, gameplay), statuses, sources, disagreements and ignored-message reasons. The prompt distinguishes same-author corrections from disagreement between players, preserves unusual fantasy details, and rejects instructions that override the application. Empty chat skips this call.
3. `set/intent.ts` validates the response before storage. Every message must be accounted for; source and preference IDs must be valid; conflicts must reference distinct active preferences. Application code derives distinct supporters and contributors from cited messages. Repetition is not an additional vote. These verified signals accompany the saved intent into world planning.
4. The world planner receives extracted intent instead of raw chat. Its `preferencePlan` must account for every preference exactly once as incorporated, adapted or omitted. Superseded and unsupported preferences must be omitted. Accepted preferences need concrete world and card expressions. Player intent guides the random world direction within the fixed format; the direction fills gaps. Conflicts require an honest explanation, not a claim that incompatible requests were both satisfied.
5. Slots reference accepted preferences through `preferenceIds`. A deterministic coverage check blocks writing if an accepted subject or gameplay preference has no assigned slot. Premise, tone and avoidance are global guidance. Unknown, superseded or omitted assignments are rejected by the slot parser.
6. Writers and revisers receive global guidance plus the subjects/gameplay assigned to their current slots. Contextual reviewers receive the accepted plan and must inspect actual expression rather than trust the planner’s claim. The full frozen blueprint remains available for cohesion. Raw conversation is never injected into card generation or review.

`setRuns` saves the conversation and parsed intent. Scheduled jobs retain generation keys. Transport/schema repairs reuse the original task. A fresh candidate reuses completed interpretation and the frozen conversation while allocating a new world direction. There are no model calls on individual chat messages and no added approval step. Existing saved blueprints and offline inputs without intent retain their earlier contracts; Default’s explicit legacy rollback does not run interpretation. Preview and release-evaluation tools support the new flow.

## Verification and limits

Public backend tests exercise member access, reaction ownership, idempotence, cross-lobby rejection, start-time freezing, a 45-message conversation, stale keys, malformed provenance repair, propagation, unassigned-subject blocking, and reuse on a fresh candidate. Parser tests cover source accounting, invalid conflicts, distinct support, omitted assignments and scoped writer guidance.

Two browser sessions (ChatAna21 and ChatBen21) posted ideas, supported the same message, refreshed, removed one reaction, read the same saved summary, and received fifteen cards each using fake services. Source quotes and one remaining supporter persisted after refresh; support controls disappeared after start. No browser errors were reported. Screenshots are in `.artifacts/chat-guidance-browser/`. Fake lore/cards are fixtures, not semantic evidence.

The real Luna probe in `.artifacts/chat-guidance-20261021/` ran three interpretation cases and one world with 135 slot assignments. All thirteen calls passed on their first attempt; total reported model cost was $0.0200074. Interpretation took 3.9–7.1 seconds in these short cases. It preserved a same-author correction from cities on moth backs to cities beneath wings, kept another player’s dragon preference as an unresolved disagreement, and identified an unsupported artifact-free request and a prompt-injection message. The resulting Underwing Reach world retained the corrected moth premise and excluded commerce-driven conflict. Every slot passed its contract.

This small probe generated no cards or art. “No dragons” was classified as a premise instead of avoidance; it remained an active global constraint and explicit conflict. Interpretation and semantic compliance remain model judgments; provenance checks cannot prove that a summary is faithful or an influence is truly expressed. Coverage proves an assignment exists, not that a printed card fulfills it. The paid probe predates the final deterministic support-signal addition, which has unit and public integration coverage. Its frozen source hashes are preserved; use a new output directory to rerun `scripts/evaluate-chat-guidance.ts` after code changes. The previous full-set quality failures and human playtest requirements remain open.
