# Card art on OpenAI's image API

Status: implemented on `convex-rewrite`, not yet deployed to production
Date: 2026-10-08

## Decision

Card art is generated by `gpt-image-2.5-flare` at `low` quality, 1008x656, JPEG, from a structured prompt built in `convex/draft/artPrompt.ts`. getimg.ai is no longer used, so the app has one AI vendor and one key.

## Model choice

The models this account can call, from `GET /v1/models` on 2026-10-08:

| Model | Released | OpenAI's positioning |
|---|---|---|
| `gpt-image-2.5-flare` | 2026-09 | "Our fastest model for high-quality, everyday image generation" |
| `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` | 2026-09 | Recommended for editing precision |
| `gpt-image-2` | 2026-04 | Previous flagship |
| `gpt-image-1.5` | 2025-11 | "Keep for existing validated workflows" |
| `gpt-image-1-mini` | 2025-09 | Cheapest per token; for batch variants and previews |
| `gpt-image-1` | 2025-04 | Legacy; third-party pricing pages list it as being retired on 2026-10-23 |

Prices are per million tokens. The 2.5 models and `gpt-image-2` are $5 text in and $30 image out; `gpt-image-1-mini` is $2 and $8.

### Measured

One prompt (a glass knight kneeling on a frozen sea), one image per row, run in parallel on 2026-10-08. Cost is computed from the token usage each response reported.

| Model | Size | Quality | Time | Output tokens | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-image-2.5-flare` | 1008x656 | low | 11.5 s | 107 | $0.0039 |
| `gpt-image-2.5-flare` | 1008x656 | medium | 13.9 s | 256 | $0.0084 |
| `gpt-image-2` | 1008x656 | low | 14.5 s | 107 | $0.0039 |
| `gpt-image-1-mini` | 1536x1024 | low | 10.3 s | 400 | $0.0035 |
| `gpt-image-1-mini` | 1536x1024 | medium | 14.8 s | 1568 | $0.0128 |

What the images showed:

- **`gpt-image-2.5-flare` at low** followed the whole prompt: glass armour, cracked visor, shards rising, a frozen sea of fractured glass, a painterly finish, no text or border.
- **Medium was slightly cleaner** but not visibly better once shrunk to the 222-pixel art box, at twice the cost.
- **`gpt-image-1-mini` at low** was muddy, lost the glass entirely, and painted open water instead of a frozen sea. It costs about the same per image as flare at low, because it needs four times the output tokens at the smallest size it supports.

So the newest model at its lowest quality is both the best and effectively the cheapest option. `sunburst` was not tested because the app never edits images.

### Size

The art box on a card is about 222 by 144 pixels, a ratio of 1.54. The 2.x models accept custom sizes whose edges are multiples of 16 and whose area is at least 655,360 pixels. 1008x656 is the smallest size that fits those rules at the card's ratio (1.537), and smaller means fewer output tokens.

## Prompting

From OpenAI's image prompting guide and its GPT Image cookbook, and what they mean here:

| Guidance | How the prompt applies it |
|---|---|
| Name the subject and intended use first | Opens with "Fantasy trading card illustration." |
| Use labelled sections for complex requests: scene, subject, details, constraints | `Scene`, `World`, `Medium`, `Tradition`, `Lighting`, `Palette`, `Detail`, `Mood`, `Composition`, `Constraints`, one per paragraph |
| "Name materials, lighting, colors, and the visual medium" | Separate lines name the paint handling, the light source, and the pigments |
| Specify scale, atmosphere, and colour instead of mood words | The card's own art direction supplies the scene; the fixed lines avoid words like "epic" or "stunning" |
| Specify framing and viewpoint | "Wide landscape crop. One subject, large in the frame ... against a plain or barely suggested background" |
| "State exclusions such as unwanted text, logos, or watermarks" | "No text, letters, borders, card frame, signature, or watermark" |
| Repeat the constraints that must stay the same across a series | Every card gets the same medium, detail, mood, composition, and world lines; only the tradition varies |
| Prefer a skimmable template to clever syntax | The prompt is a fixed template with two inputs |

### The look: pulp

The art direction went through three versions on 2026-10-08 and 09, each judged from sample images:

1. **One style line** ("1970s pulp fantasy paperback covers ... a saturated but earthy palette"). Competent but generic.
2. **Classical schools** (Romantic, Baroque, Golden Age, Pre-Raphaelite, Symbolist) with dramatic directional lighting. Rejected as too detailed, too intense, and too heroic.
3. **Five pulp cover traditions, one per set.** The right look, but too little variety.
4. **28 traditions, one at random per card**, the current version. This returns to how the 2024 system worked, which appended one of five style lines to each card at random, but with a much larger pool and a shared pulp direction holding it together.

The prompt now has these lines after the scene and world:

- **Medium** is fixed: a small, quickly made, hand-made illustration for a cheap paperback, pulp magazine, or storybook, with big simple shapes and unfinished corners. Explicitly not digital.
- **Tradition, lighting, and palette** come from one of the 28 schools in `convex/draft/artSchools.ts`. Each names the illustrators it draws on, a kind of light, and three or four colours.
- **Detail** tells the model what to leave out: ornament, particles, sparks, and unrequested glows. Image models add these by default, and naming them was what brought the detail level down.
- **Mood** asks for a quiet, ordinary, or odd moment and rules out heroic poses and epic scale.

`pickSchool` maps a random number to a school; the generation action supplies the number, once per card. The world line still carries the setting into every image, so a set shares its subject matter while its cards differ in hand.

On artist names: the schools name illustrators such as Margaret Brundage, Frank Frazetta, Richard Powers, Arthur Rackham, Moebius, and Quinton Hoover as shorthand for a tradition. All are deceased, and the API accepted all 28 prompts. The prompt does not name living artists, which drops Brom and Michael Whelan from the 2024 prompts.

One sample of every school, all painting the same scene, is in `card-art-traditions.html`. The longer prompt raises the cost per image from about $0.0039 to about $0.0052.

While generating those 28 samples, one request returned a 500 from OpenAI and succeeded when repeated. The gateway now retries server errors as well as rate limits, which is the likely cause of the one missing image in the earlier real draft.

## What it costs and how long it takes

| | Images | Art cost |
|---|---|---|
| One pack | 12 | about $0.06 |
| Two-player draft (3 rounds) | 72 | about $0.37 |
| Eight-player draft | 288 | about $1.50 |

Packs are topped up to fifteen with copies of existing cards, which reuse their art, so a pack is always twelve images.

A real two-player round on 2026-10-08 took 110 seconds from Start to packs on screen, covering the setting, two packs of cards from `gpt-4o`, and 24 images. 23 of the 24 images arrived; one card was saved without art and the reason was not recorded. Failures are now logged with their status code.

## Limits

- **Rate limits will bite at larger tables.** OpenAI's lowest tier allows 20 images a minute. An eight-player round is 96 images, so it cannot finish in under about five minutes at that tier, against a ten-minute limit on a Convex action. The response headers did not reveal this account's tier.
- **How the code copes.** Each pack paints four images at a time. A request that is rate limited or hits a server error waits for the `retry-after` the API gives (ten seconds if none) and tries up to six times. An image that still fails, or fails for any other reason, leaves that card without art; the draft is never blocked on art.
- **Content filtering is on its default.** A request the filter refuses comes back as an error and that card has no art. The API has a `moderation: "low"` setting that was accepted in a test but is not used.

## Not done

- No retry later for cards that ended up without art.
- No use of the Batch API, which halves the image price but is not suitable for a live draft.
- The card-design calls are still `gpt-4o` with the 2024 prompts; that is roadmap step 2.
