# 06 — Case studies

[Research index](README.md) · Next: [Implications for DraftGPT](07-draftgpt-implications.md)

These examples demonstrate different structures. “Released” below means the official guide's description of the released set, not a claim that every deck was equally powerful. “Handoff” describes an earlier proposal. Lessons labeled synthesis are our interpretation.

## Theros Beyond Death: several decks from shared themes

Rosewater's account derives multiple archetypes from enchantments and graveyards. GW uses enchantments aggressively, WU combines them with evasive creatures, and GU takes a slower ramp approach. BG and UB use the graveyard differently. Other pairs extend existing support or receive focused themes, such as RW's heroic-style rewards and RG's high-power creatures. [Limited themes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-12-part-2-limited-themes-2020-03-16).

**Synthesis:** ten decks do not need ten unrelated resources. Different curves, rewards, and ways to end the game can make one shared category support several distinct experiences.

## Strixhaven: reinterpret the same colors

The 2021 design story presents five colleges united by spellcasting while distinguishing their behavior:

| College | Colors | Mechanical emphasis in the design account |
|---|---|---|
| Silverquill | WB | Aggression, creature enhancement, evasion |
| Prismari | UR | Larger spells |
| Witherbloom | BG | Life as a resource and reward |
| Lorehold | RW | The past expressed through the graveyard |
| Quandrix | GU | Growing creatures and resources |

Shared magecraft triggers could produce different outputs for each college. [In the Strixhaven, Part 2](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/strixhaven-part-2-2021-04-05).

Quintorius's design history shows a draft build-around changing card type, stats, and rewards as that identity developed. A resonant concept did not arrive fully formed. [School's in Session](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/schools-session-2021-04-12).

The 2026 Secrets of Strixhaven handoff revisits Lorehold's challenge: finding natural support for a slower RW graveyard strategy. It discusses an artifact direction and extra mascot text that were subsequently removed. [Secrets handoff, Part 1](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/secrets-of-strixhaven-vision-design-handoff-part-1).

**Synthesis:** reinterpreting a pair is possible, but it creates a support problem to solve. A surprising label alone cannot make the necessary cards exist.

## Bloomburrow: ten animal identities without ten identical decks

The released guide describes these identities:

| Pair | Animal | Gameplay focus |
|---|---|---|
| WU | Birds | Flying creatures helping grounded allies |
| UB | Rats | Graveyard threshold, controlling play |
| BR | Lizards | Opponent life loss and pressure |
| RG | Raccoons | Large mana expenditures |
| GW | Rabbits | Numerous creatures and tokens |
| WB | Bats | Life gained and lost |
| UR | Otters | Instants, sorceries, prowess |
| BG | Squirrels | Food and graveyard resources |
| RW | Mice | Targeting creatures, valiant |
| GU | Frogs | Repeated creature entry |

The guide also identifies dual-type creatures and hybrid cards as ways to combine support. [Bloomburrow Prerelease Guide](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/bloomburrow-prerelease-guide).

The handoff's design goal explicitly avoided making every animal deck a collection of small creatures plus a team bonus. It considered different speeds and resource patterns. Its prototype Rats were faster than the released guide's description. One handoff table also labels Squirrels blue-green; the surrounding material and release guide establish black-green. Do not copy that apparent table typo. [Handoff, Part 3](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/bloomburrow-vision-design-handoff-part-3).

The earlier fellowship mechanic attempted to make creature-type rewards work across multiple animal types. The handoff explains the supply problem: ten animals did not leave enough room for everyone to draft an exclusively single-species deck. Fellowship was explored as a solution, then removed during later design. [Handoff, Part 2](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/bloomburrow-vision-design-handoff-part-2).

**Synthesis:** strong identities need connecting cards. A shared style or species map cannot substitute for enough playable combinations. Also, a proposed mechanic's removal can preserve the design goal while simplifying its execution.

## Duskmourn: two broad themes, ten expressions

The released guide identifies enchantments and the graveyard as major themes:

| Pair | Intended strategy |
|---|---|
| WU | Eerie tempo |
| UB | Eerie control |
| BR | Sacrifice, including Rooms |
| RG | Aggressive delirium creatures |
| GW | Survival and tapped creatures |
| WB | Reanimation |
| UR | Rooms |
| BG | Slower delirium value |
| RW | Small-creature aggression |
| GU | Manifest dread |

The same broad resource can contribute to multiple decks, while the payoff changes. [Duskmourn Prerelease and Draft Guide](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/duskmourn-house-of-horror-prerelease-and-draft-guide).

**Synthesis:** compare UR here with UR Otters. A color pair's stable vocabulary does not require the same deck every time. Also compare WU/UB and RG/BG within Duskmourn: shared mechanics can support different pacing.

## Tarkir: Dragonstorm: two-color starts, three-color destinations

The released guide centers five three-color clans, with additional strategies in shared pairs. Abzan is WBG, Jeskai URW, Sultai BGU, Mardu RWB, and Temur GUR. [Tarkir Prerelease Guide](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/tarkir-dragonstorm-prerelease-guide).

Wizards' design account explains the structural advantage of starting in enemy pairs, each of which can lead into two wedges. [A Dragonstorm Is Brewing, Part 1](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/a-dragonstorm-is-brewing-part-1).

**Derived transition map:**

| Starting pair | Add one color to reach |
|---|---|
| WB | G → Abzan; R → Mardu |
| UR | W → Jeskai; G → Temur |
| BG | W → Abzan; U → Sultai |
| RW | U → Jeskai; B → Mardu |
| GU | B → Sultai; R → Temur |

This table is a deduction from the clan colors. It shows optionality; it does not claim a player must follow one route or that every route has equal strength.

## New Capenna: coherent presentation does not guarantee gameplay

The 2022 retrospective praises the plane's aesthetic while reporting insufficient faction distinction and a world too narrowly centered on crime. It separately identifies aggressive monocolor commons, color imbalance, and swingy rares as problems for its intended three-color draft experience. [State of Design 2022](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/state-of-design-2022).

**Synthesis:** assess creative breadth and gameplay incentives independently, then check their agreement. A set can look distinctive and still fail to produce its promised decks. A mechanically functional faction can still feel indistinguishable from its neighbors.
