# Rare power references

[Guide and methodology](../10-modern-horizons-power.md) · [Machine-readable data](cards.json)

Fetched 2026-10-16. Bands are editorial judgments for the specified context. The card links establish printed facts, not our classification. Read the full costs and support requirements before applying a verdict.

## Creature

### Too Strong: [Nadu, Winged Wisdom](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/193/nadu-winged-wisdom)

**{1}{G}{U}; 3/4 · Legendary Creature — Bird Wizard · MH3 #193 · printed rare.**

**Role:** target-triggered resource engine. **Effect summary:** A three-mana flying creature grants creatures target-triggered land-or-card value, limited separately for each creature each turn.

**Why this band:** Above the ceiling with repeatable cheap targeting: per-creature limits and new creatures do not meaningfully bound the engine. Evaluate the entire trigger network, not just the word twice.

**Costs and context:** The documented constructed failure used Shuko or Outrider en-Kor, then more complex loops. A draft set without targeting enablers is a different context.

**Additional context:** [source 1](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/on-banning-nadu-winged-wisdom-in-modern). These sources do not establish an objective rating for this custom format.

### Target: [Psychic Frog](https://scryfall.com/card/mh3/199/psychic-frog)

**{U}{B}; 1/2 · Creature — Frog · MH3 #199 · printed rare.**

**Role:** cheap evasive card engine. **Effect summary:** A two-mana creature draws on player or planeswalker combat damage, grows by discarding cards, and gains flying by exiling graveyard cards.

**Why this band:** An ambitious but appropriate rare target for a supported blue-black lane: pressure, resource conversion, and card advantage on a cheap interactive body.

**Costs and context:** Needs combat connection; growth consumes cards and flying consumes graveyard material. Its Legacy ban does not automatically make the design an invalid high-powered Limited reference.

**Additional context:** [source 1](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/banned-and-restricted-december-16-2024). These sources do not establish an objective rating for this custom format.

### Too Weak: [Wood Elemental](https://scryfall.com/card/leg/215/wood-elemental)

**{3}{G}; */* · Creature — Elemental · LEG #215 · printed rare.**

**Role:** expensive resource-consuming creature. **Effect summary:** A four-mana creature requires sacrificing untapped Forests on entry; those sacrifices determine its size.

**Why this band:** An obvious lower bound: development is paid twice, first in mana and then in lands, for a body with no compensating impact or protection.

**Costs and context:** Land-sacrifice synergies rarely recover the immediate tempo loss. Do not treat printed mana as the full cost.

## Instant

### Too Strong: [Ancestral Recall](https://scryfall.com/card/lea/47/ancestral-recall)

**{U} · Instant · LEA #47 · printed rare.**

**Role:** cheap card advantage. **Effect summary:** One blue mana lets a target player draw three cards.

**Why this band:** Far above the ceiling: two net cards for a single mana with no setup, leaving the rest of the turn available for development or interaction.

**Costs and context:** This is a deliberately extreme upper bound, not a close comparison for every draw spell.

### Target: [Force of Negation](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/52/force-of-negation)

**{1}{U}{U} · Instant · MH1 #52 · printed rare.**

**Role:** free defensive interaction. **Effect summary:** Counters and exiles a noncreature spell; outside your turn, a blue card can be exiled instead of paying three mana.

**Why this band:** A suitable rare efficiency anchor: zero-mana defense is allowed when it costs another card, has a timing gate, and misses creatures.

**Costs and context:** Free use spends two cards to answer one. Evaluate blue-card density and the set's noncreature threats; it is not a universally efficient two-for-one.

### Too Weak: [Pale Moon](https://scryfall.com/card/nem/36/pale-moon)

**{1}{U} · Instant · NEM #36 · printed rare.**

**Role:** temporary mana disruption. **Effect summary:** Two-mana instant makes nonbasic lands produce only colorless mana for the rest of the turn.

**Why this band:** A narrow one-turn inconvenience is not enough for a premium rare interaction slot. It neither removes a threat nor reliably denies the opponent a useful action.

**Costs and context:** An opponent can often float colored mana in response, and basics are unaffected. Nonbasic-heavy combo matchups are special cases.

## Sorcery

### Too Strong: [Balance](https://scryfall.com/card/lea/3/balance)

**{1}{W} · Sorcery · LEA #3 · printed rare.**

**Role:** asymmetric reset enabler. **Effect summary:** Two mana reduces players' lands, hands, and creatures to the smallest respective counts through sacrifice and discard.

**Why this band:** Above the ceiling when a deck converts resources into unaffected permanents: the nominal symmetry can become a cheap one-sided reset.

**Costs and context:** Requires tracking what each player actually loses. Artifact/enchantment-heavy boards and emptied hands break the symmetry; it is not literally a one-sided spell.

### Target: [Damn](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/80/damn)

**{B}{B} · Sorcery · MH2 #80 · printed rare.**

**Role:** spot removal with a sweeper mode. **Effect summary:** Two black mana destroys a creature; four-mana overload instead destroys every creature.

**Why this band:** A desirable rare combines efficient early interaction with a major late board option. Flexibility creates rare value without making the baseline weak.

**Costs and context:** The single-target and overload modes require different colored commitments. The sweeper also destroys your creatures.

### Too Weak: [Morality Shift](https://scryfall.com/card/jud/70/morality-shift)

**{5}{B}{B} · Sorcery · JUD #70 · printed rare.**

**Role:** large graveyard setup. **Effect summary:** Seven mana exchanges its controller's library and graveyard.

**Why this band:** Too little immediate board or resource access for a normal expensive spell slot. It asks for another payoff after consuming most of a turn.

**Costs and context:** Dedicated graveyard wins can exploit it. Such a package must exist in the actual set before this is credited as a finisher.

## Enchantment

### Too Strong: [Opposition](https://scryfall.com/card/uds/40/opposition)

**{2}{U}{U} · Enchantment · UDS #40 · printed rare.**

**Role:** repeatable tap and mana-denial engine. **Effect summary:** Four-mana enchantment lets creatures tap to tap an artifact, creature, or land.

**Why this band:** Above our ceiling in a token-rich environment: spare creatures can deny mana before main phases as well as clear blockers, turning a board lead into a soft lock.

**Costs and context:** Needs a creature board and can be attacked by removal or sweepers. Tapping only blockers without land denial would be a different design.

### Target: [Bitterblossom](https://scryfall.com/card/mor/58/bitterblossom)

**{1}{B} · Kindred Enchantment — Faerie · MOR #58 · printed rare.**

**Role:** low-cost flying token engine. **Effect summary:** Two-mana enchantment creates a flying Faerie each upkeep at the cost of one life.

**Why this band:** A real rare engine can accumulate evasive material cheaply. It is acceptable to gain repeated value when the first token is delayed and life loss creates a race.

**Costs and context:** No immediate body, continued life loss, and enchantment answers matter. Sacrifice or tribal packages can push it higher.

### Too Weak: [Conjured Currency](https://scryfall.com/card/rtr/33/conjured-currency)

**{5}{U} · Enchantment · RTR #33 · printed rare.**

**Role:** delayed permanent exchange. **Effect summary:** Six-mana enchantment can trade control of itself for an eligible permanent at each upkeep of its current controller.

**Why this band:** Below target: no entry impact, a full-turn delay, and the opponent receives the same exchange tool. It does not reliably secure the resource it appears to steal.

**Costs and context:** Ownership restrictions affect eligible targets. A carefully engineered board can improve it, but ordinary use is slow and unreliable.

## Artifact

### Too Strong: [Umezawa's Jitte](https://scryfall.com/card/bok/163/umezawas-jitte)

**{2} · Legendary Artifact — Equipment · BOK #163 · printed rare.**

**Role:** equipment combat-control engine. **Effect summary:** Two-mana Equipment gains counters from combat damage; counters provide removal, pump, or life. Equip costs two.

**Why this band:** Above our combat ceiling: the first connection finances future removal and makes subsequent combat increasingly unfavorable. Multiple resource modes snowball from normal attacks or blocks.

**Costs and context:** Full initial investment is four mana plus a creature. Killing the carrier or artifact remains possible, but the accumulated counters make racing and small creatures much worse.

### Target: [Nettlecyst](https://scryfall.com/card/mh2/231/nettlecyst)

**{3} · Artifact — Equipment · MH2 #231 · printed rare.**

**Role:** artifact/enchantment scaling threat. **Effect summary:** Three-mana living-weapon Equipment supplies a Germ and scales its bonus with controlled artifacts and enchantments; later equip costs two.

**Why this band:** A strong rare build-around supplies its own body and rewards the deck's ordinary permanents. This is a much better expensive Equipment pattern than paying twice before obtaining any creature.

**Costs and context:** Count the Equipment itself; Germ dies if its final toughness is zero. Actual artifact/enchantment density controls the ceiling.

### Too Weak: [Jayemdae Tome](https://scryfall.com/card/7ed/305/jayemdae-tome)

**{4} · Artifact — Book · 7ED #305 · printed rare.**

**Role:** repeatable card draw artifact. **Effect summary:** Four mana to cast, then four mana and tapping to draw each card.

**Why this band:** The first card costs eight total mana and only replaces the Tome. Repeatability does not excuse a huge initial tempo deficit in this environment.

**Costs and context:** A very slow control game can eventually repay the investment. Compare that timetable to immediate cantrips and draw engines attached to threats.

## Land

### Too Strong: [Tolarian Academy](https://scryfall.com/card/usg/330/tolarian-academy)

**Land — no mana cost · Legendary Land · USG #330 · printed rare.**

**Role:** artifact-funded fast mana land. **Effect summary:** A legendary land taps for blue mana equal to the number of controlled artifacts.

**Why this band:** Above the ceiling with cheap artifacts: a land drop produces multiple mana without an activation investment. Artifact tokens can accelerate the engine further.

**Costs and context:** Conditional ceiling: it produces nothing without artifacts. Rare and mythic printings are both shown; neither rarity makes a dense artifact shell safe.

### Target: [Prismatic Vista](https://scryfall.com/card/mh1/244/prismatic-vista)

**Land — no mana cost · Land · MH1 #244 · printed rare.**

**Role:** flexible untapped basic fixing. **Effect summary:** A land sacrifices with one life to find a basic land, which enters untapped.

**Why this band:** Appropriate premium fixing: any basic color on demand without a tempo delay, paid for with life and restricted to basic targets.

**Costs and context:** Does not fetch duals merely because they have basic land types. Landfall and graveyard support add value; basics must be available.

### Too Weak: [Wintermoon Mesa](https://scryfall.com/card/pcy/143/wintermoon-mesa)

**Land — no mana cost · Land · PCY #143 · printed rare.**

**Role:** slow land with one-shot disruption. **Effect summary:** Enters tapped, produces colorless, and can spend two mana plus sacrifice to tap two lands.

**Why this band:** Weak premium land: an entry penalty and no fixing buy only temporary disruption with another activation cost and loss of the land.

**Costs and context:** Specific denial strategies can use it. A routine draft deck should prefer colored sources or more reliable utility.
